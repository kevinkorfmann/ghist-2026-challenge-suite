# GHIST 2026 methods — ancestrum

## Author and participation

Kevin Korfmann (Synapse username **ancestrum**), individual participant. No institutional affiliation is asserted for this entry.

This writeup describes the methods used for the fourteen final challenge entries prepared on 20 November 2026. The source repository is [ghist-2026-challenge-suite](https://github.com/kevinkorfmann/ghist-2026-challenge-suite). Task-specific source snapshots, configuration files, hashes, input provenance, diagnostics, failed runs and submission corrections are retained in local or versioned evidence archives; not all assets are publicly uploaded. Raw challenge genotypes, sequencing reads and credentials are not reproduced here.

These entries are best efforts under the competition deadline. Passing file-format, provenance or numerical checks does not establish scientific accuracy. Task 9 has a separately validated ten-label release; the other methods retain failed or incomplete scientific qualification checks. Historical testing results in the repository should not be interpreted as current validation: subsequent review identified simulation/extraction mismatches, scaling problems and truth-assisted localization in some historical evaluations. Those results were retained as audit evidence rather than silently replaced. The final prediction procedures did not use final truth or score-based biological retuning. The server-driven output corrections described below were made after format rejection, without refitting models.

## Challenges 1–4: demographic inference

We estimated demographic parameters from site-frequency spectra using the repository's moments/dadi fitting implementations. Input identity, ancestral-state orientation, population membership, contig lengths and extraction settings were checked before fitting. Physical parameter conversion used the task-specific mutation rates and actual analyzed sequence exposure. Tasks 1 and 2 used 100,000,000 bp; Tasks 3 and 4 used 61,965,066 bp in the fixed noncoding 1–100 kb distance strata, calculated from the supplied coding annotation and contig lengths. The complete final plan contained 48 fit cases: 34 returned finite verified outputs and 14 failed numerical integration. All cases were terminal before selection. We retained failures, optimizer status and the fixed likelihood domain. We submitted point estimates without confidence intervals.

### Challenge 1 — bottleneck

The primary estimator was a folded 114-haplotype two-epoch moments fit with sixteen original starts and a paired coarse/fine/refinement optimization strategy. An eight-start dadi fit was retained as sensitivity analysis; the primary engine was fixed before inference. The selected moments result converged. Repeated VCF positions were retained as separate original records after correcting an overly strict preflight assumption; no deduplication, allele replacement or coordinate displacement was applied. Recovery under misspecification and interval coverage remain unresolved. Relevant implementation and evidence are in the bottleneck modules, `docs/bottleneck_optimizer_production_2026_11_20.md`, and `experiments/final_submission_2026_11_20/demography/`.

### Challenge 2 — secondary contact

We fitted the existing simple and historical `generator_matched` secondary-contact models to the same unfolded 40×30 joint spectrum, each with five free parameters, optimized theta and eight original starts. Population 1 was mainland and population 2 island. The locked choice was the highest finite multinomial likelihood on this common domain, with deterministic ties. Contact time was exported as migration time. The selected model was `generator_matched` (start r06), which includes fixed hidden epochs whose validity for the final generator is unverified. The selected finite endpoint reported `ABNORMAL` optimizer termination; we did not suppress that status or claim stationarity. The task therefore remains an unqualified estimate despite valid finite output. See `scripts/fit_secondary_contact_spectrum_archive.py` and the demography method lock and selection audit.

### Challenge 3 — growth with background selection

Exponential growth was the fixed primary model. We fitted projections 84, 60 and 40 and reported componentwise medians of the physical parameters. All three primary fits converged. Three sudden-growth fits were retained as sensitivity analyses and did not replace the predeclared primary. Background selection, demographic convention and parameter identifiability limit interpretation; a median across projections is an aggregation rule rather than an independent estimate of uncertainty. Source and diagnostics are in the growth-with-background-selection modules, `scripts/compare_growth_models.py`, and the demography archive.

### Challenge 4 — split and migration with background selection

We ran eight original starts at each projection 50×40, 70×50 and 94×68. Within each projection, the maximum finite likelihood was selected; likelihood values were not compared across projections. The submitted parameters were componentwise physical medians, with AFR preceding EUR. No historical learned correction was applied. The three selected fits had convergence flags true, false and false; the two unconverged endpoints remain explicitly recorded. Eleven of the twenty-four fit cases failed fixed-domain integration checks. The median need not correspond to a single fitted mechanistic parameter vector. See the split/migration modules and `experiments/final_submission_2026_11_20/demography/README.md`.

## Challenges 5–8: selective sweeps

We scanned the actual current final chromosome 9 VCFs with the fixed accessibility mask. Methods were frozen before final inference, and localization was genome-wide without access to true selected positions. Outputs are zero-based, half-open BED intervals. Independent checks verified input/source hashes, contig and callable bounds, interval geometry and output counts. Historical truth-assisted localization results are not offered as validation for these blind final scans. The methods and raw diagnostics are in `src/ghist_single_sweep/`, the corresponding prediction scripts, and `experiments/final_submission_2026_11_20/sweeps_takeover/`.

### Challenge 5 — single hard sweep

The fixed method takes the median of three location estimates: a full-scan 100 kb composite maximum with 20 kb steps, the largest variant-gap midpoint, and a 100 kb flank-LD estimate. The composite combines robust standardized diversity, site-density, Tajima D, H12 and LD statistics. Local refinement uses a 750 kb radius around the full-scan maximum. The submitted interval has a fixed 60 kb half-width. The final three-estimator span was approximately 165.8 kb, exceeding the predeclared 50 kb consensus criterion; the candidate was submitted as a best effort despite that failure. RAiSD was not used by this task's selected predictor. See `scripts/predict_single_sweep.py` and the retained raw prediction JSON.

### Challenge 6 — single soft sweep

We used the previously selected development method maximizing H12 over 50-SNP windows at 12-SNP steps, with no minimum minor-frequency cutoff. Fully callable window spans of at most 500 kb were eligible; exact maximum ties use the first genomic window. The selected center was packaged with a fixed 75 kb half-width. This method lacks independent confirmation after development selection; H12 localization and the submitted width should not be interpreted as a calibrated confidence interval. See the soft-sweep development/prediction code and the final runner's method lock.

### Challenge 7 — single hard sweep with background selection

The fixed estimate is the median of ten locations: composite maxima at five physical window scales (25, 50, 100, 250 and 500 kb), a variant-gap midpoint, flank-LD estimates at 50, 100 and 250 kb, and RAiSD with a 50-SNP window. RAiSD windows crossing inaccessible sequence are rejected. The output has an 80 kb half-width. The final 500 kb composite estimate strongly disagreed with the other locations, so the complete consensus-span criterion failed. We preserved the frozen median rather than deleting a disagreeing estimator. Matched biological recovery remains absent. See `scripts/predict_task7_sweep_bgs.py`.

### Challenge 8 — multiple sweeps with background selection

We transferred a provisional RAiSD threshold from the completed Task 7 reference: twice its distant-background peak score, giving 29.9. Passing candidates were separated by at least 1 Mb and snapped to the nearest callable exonic base within 60 kb and represented by fixed 60 kb half-widths. Two intervals were emitted; the fallback path was unused. This threshold has no calibrated null distribution and no independent multiple-sweep recovery qualification. The official two-column exon annotation was interpreted as 1-based inclusive and converted to BED; its source documentation did not explicitly establish that convention, leaving a one-base boundary uncertainty. See the multiple-sweep predictor and final raw prediction records.

## Challenge 9 — exact IBD segment pairs

A frozen random forest classifies relationships from exact Ped-sim IBD1/IBD2 segments. Its 218 fixed features include segment counts and lengths, distribution summaries and histograms, chromosome sharing, IBD2 proportions, and sex-specific map-derived lengths. The model used 600 trees, Gini splitting, bootstrap sampling, balanced-subsample class weights, `max_features=0.8`, minimum leaf size 2 and seed 90926. The ten-label refit used 60,266 training pairs after removing retired labels without tuning against the new confirmations.

Two distinct untouched confirmation datasets contained 10,038 matched-interference pairs and 9,985 Poisson-crossover-shift pairs. Exact label accuracy was 89.10% and 87.20%; balanced accuracy was 83.35% and 81.48%. These results condition on observed exact IBD and the simulated pedigree distributions. Fourth- and fifth-cousin distinctions remained difficult, and forest probabilities are not calibrated posteriors. The final file covers all 997 observed unordered pairs from 39,916 input segments. There is no separately supplied zero-IBD roster in this input, so coverage is defined by observed pairs. This does not validate genotype processing or detection errors. The release, source, runtime and map are pinned in `configs/task9_current_release.json` and `requirements/task9-release-2026-11-20.txt`; further methods are in `experiments/final_submission_2026_11_20/task9/task9_final_methods.md`.

## Challenge 10 — large human family

The final method uses hap-IBD on phased genotypes, a frozen classifier trained on detected IBD, and structural decoding. Haplotype edges are converted to diploid IBD states using genuine homolog matching. The pair model supplies probability-weighted meiosis distances; neighbor joining proposes a topology, positive integer edge lengths are refined under a fixed bounded search, and valid roots are ranked by composite pair-model scores. These root scores are not posterior probabilities. The policy permits a whole-cohort pair-model fallback only for the specifically recognized initial neighbor-joining failure to find an accepted root; that fallback was not used for the final 263-person input. All 34,453 unordered pairs were retained.

The selected policy followed multiple disclosed development failures. In two fresh 229-person cohorts, structural exact accuracy was 67.41% and 68.13%, with pooled weighted Jaccard 0.5244; the prospective practical exact-accuracy target of 70% failed, as did stricter scientific gates. Improvements over the corresponding pair model do not establish adequate absolute accuracy. The final entry was nevertheless the fixed best available method. Map-domain cropping and genotype preparation were bound in the execution protocol; founder-model and missingness transfer remain limitations. See `docs/task10_development_2026_11_20.md`, `scripts/develop_task10_detected_ibd.py`, and `experiments/task10_development_2026_11_20/structural_inference_handoff_protocol_v4.json`.

## Challenge 11 — inbred dog multifamily cohort

The selected corrected context ExtraTrees classifier (800 trees, minimum leaf size 3, `max_features=0.75`, seed 20300111) combines detected IBD summaries, explicit HBD masking, homolog-sharing features and inferred-family context. Families are clustered with the fixed dog-map pipeline. Context includes union sharing over predicted sibling groups and regions where two predicted siblings both share with a third individual while not sharing detected IBD with one another. No true family labels, sample-name encodings or pedigree properties are used as predictor features. The earlier Bonsai consensus that degraded correct sibling calls was not the selected final decision policy.

The corrected data split contained 108 training, 54 calibration and 54 development cohorts. The final choice was made among four fixed candidates on the same 54 corrected synthetic development cohorts, using a prospectively specified weighted-Jaccard comparison. The corrected context model achieved 6,535/7,305 exact labels (89.46%), macro recall 80.81% and weighted Jaccard 0.8350, with all 432 families recovered. These are development-selection results, not independent post-selection confirmation. Half-avuncular recall was 57.05% in this development comparison and 41.98% in calibration, failing the 60% strict threshold. The final input was cropped to the fixed training-map support before inference, with original inputs retained. The selected context model used temperature 0.75. See `scripts/develop_task11_pedigree.py`, `docs/task11_development_2026_11_20.md`, and `experiments/task11_development_2026_11_20/pragmatic_corrected_context_candidate_v1.json`.

## Challenge 12 — human family with background relatedness

We retained the historical `empirical_background_v1` classifier and its `task12-top20-v1` correction, with quantile 0.25, minimum 5 cM and corrected weight 1. The initial strict run stopped on missing genotypes. The separate best-effort preparation completed 148,382 missing alleles (approximately 0.1003%) using the observed per-site major allele, preserving observed alleles and phase guards; three multiallelic sites were skipped. Such imputation can bias IBD and is not independently qualified. Hap-IBD used 1 cM seeds, 0.2 cM extension, 2 cM output, 100 markers and 1,000 bp maximum gap. The unchanged historical classifier then produced all 43,956 pairs among 297 individuals. Every pair had detected segments, so the zero-segment fallback was unused.

The historical correction retains a documented top-20 histogram truncation defect to preserve the selected model's feature semantics. No model retraining occurred on final data, and historical component accuracy is not an estimate of final-data accuracy. Full genotype-to-detector-to-family scientific qualification remains incomplete. See `docs/task12_histogram_repair_2026_11_20.md` and `experiments/submission_package_2026_11_20/task12_deadline/`.

## Challenges 13–14: low-coverage demographic inference

We used ANGSD GL2 genotype likelihoods and full-width site-allele-frequency likelihoods with minimum mapping quality 30 and base quality 20, followed by winsfs 0.7.0 and moments demographic fitting. No hard genotype calls or SNP-significance filter were applied. Spectra were folded globally, with complementary boundary bins symmetrized; physical scaling used mutation rate 1e-7 and measured all-bin callable exposure. This retains sequencing uncertainty instead of converting low-depth reads into certain diploid calls. Reference, BAM, index and population-file identities were checked; the organizer header was normalized in a separate staging copy. Spectra, optimizer starts and repeated-estimator diagnostics were retained. Both tasks use eight starts for each of L-BFGS-B and Powell. These are pragmatic methods selected after retained development failures, not qualified low-coverage releases. The exact estimator snapshot is `source_v12`, indexed by `experiments/lowcoverage_2026_11_20/source_manifest_v12.json` (SHA-256 `15725e52355e9a7138e3f345d5838518a81ca3ecb6d335accf66b4fa1286be66`); the shared pragmatic freeze has SHA-256 `7a98a0e446cf5b5221fef48bace8743b5a604f7cd807fdff0c26df3df001b1bb`. The Task 13 wrapper `scripts/lowcoverage_predict_task13_pragmatic.py` has SHA-256 `571ff1fd856789522aab29d7451ccc4c0b5f66cce9972709d1bc1ea9908be1ce`; the Task 14 wrapper `scripts/lowcoverage_predict_pragmatic.py` has SHA-256 `ef415c585e151b457b929a39fb6037165bbde92e92f86e4ecce06be3a9ba2f91`. These pin retained source files and are not claims that every snapshot has already been published to GitHub.

### Challenge 13 — low-coverage bottleneck

The fixed spectrum policy uses 500 blocks, window 100, tolerance 1e-4 and a 50-epoch cap. We ran seeds 761001 and 761002 and a continuation initialized from the first spectrum. The continuation was designated the submitted estimate in advance; outcomes did not select among seeds. Development age error was −4.32% and bottleneck-fraction error +41.21%. The final numerical target-stability gate also failed. We submitted the finite, independently checked continuation estimate with those failures preserved. See `scripts/lowcoverage_predict_task13_pragmatic.py` and `experiments/lowcoverage_2026_11_20/task13_pragmatic_inference_handoff_v1.md`.

### Challenge 14 — low-coverage split without migration

The selected method uses a globally folded joint spectrum and a moments two-population split-without-migration model. winsfs uses 500 blocks, full window 500, tolerance 1e-6 and a 500-epoch cap. Seed 761001 is the fixed primary; seed 761002 and a continuation provide numerical diagnostics. This pragmatic policy passed the retained final numerical checks. Earlier six-cohort and cap-200 policies had failed, however, and no fresh cap-500 confirmation was completed. The cap-500 development cases were previously inspected and cannot support an untouched-validation claim. No outcome-dependent seed switch or empirical bias correction was applied. See `scripts/lowcoverage_predict_pragmatic.py` and `experiments/lowcoverage_2026_11_20/task14_pragmatic_protocol_v1.json`.

## Output corrections and reproducibility

The server rejected the first Task 1–4 files because estimates needed the official YAML `parameters` wrapper. The corrected files preserve their numerical estimates exactly. For Tasks 10 and 12, the server accepted twelve human relationship labels, whereas the initial local contract permitted fourteen. The correction projected `full_1st_cousin_thrice_removed` to `full_2nd_cousin_once_removed`, and `avuncular_twice_removed` to `full_1st_cousin_once_removed`, preserving the original implied meiosis counts. These are distinct pedigree relationships: matching meiosis counts does not make them biologically equivalent. Task 10 changed 3,040 labels under the first mapping. Task 12 changed 4,275 and 3,025 labels respectively and appended deterministic `meioses_count`. All pair IDs, pair order, other labels and original files were preserved. No fitted probability, threshold or demographic estimate changed in response to scientific score feedback.

Reproducibility evidence includes immutable source/model/map hashes, fixed selection rules, full planned fit denominators, executed commands, failed starts, input metadata, output hashes, pair/coordinate checks and retained receipts. Compact repository archives omit large input files and model assets; reproducing a run requires those exact bound assets and the recorded runtime. File validation is distinct from biological validation and from a confirmed competition receipt.

## Discussion

The most useful lesson was that successful component tests and stable predictions can conceal failures in the complete inference chain. Complete-family simulations exposed damaging pedigree-consensus behavior, and final server checks exposed output-contract differences missed locally. Broadly sampled synthetic cohorts, explicit failed-run denominators, fixed selection rules and separate format versus scientific checks made those limitations visible. Future work should emphasize independent complete-pipeline confirmation, missingness and founder-model transfer, calibrated uncertainty and better sweep null calibration. Earlier versioned machine-readable server schemas and representative end-to-end examples would reduce late format corrections.
